# Story Package: Palm Sunday – Jesus’ Triumphal Entry (Matthew 21, Mark 11, Luke 19, John 12)

**Story Key**: `palmSunday`

**Position in 365 Roadmap**: Phase 6, Story #24 — The King who comes in peace and is worthy of our praise.

---

## Reverent Read-Aloud Retelling (for ages 3–8)

Jesus and His disciples were walking toward the city of Jerusalem. As they came near the Mount of Olives, Jesus sent two of His friends ahead. He told them, “Go into the village. You will find a donkey tied there with her colt. Untie them and bring them to Me. If anyone asks why, say, ‘The Lord needs them.’”

This happened just as Jesus said. The disciples brought the donkey and her colt. They put their coats on the colt for Jesus to sit on.

A very large crowd had gathered. As Jesus rode into Jerusalem, the people spread their coats on the road. Others cut branches from the palm trees and laid them down like a carpet. The crowd was so happy!

They shouted with joy, “Hosanna to the Son of David! Blessed is He who comes in the name of the Lord! Hosanna in the highest heaven!”

Some of the religious leaders were upset. They told Jesus to make the people stop shouting. But Jesus said, “If they keep quiet, the stones themselves will cry out.”

Jesus rode into the city on a humble donkey, not on a war horse. The people praised Him as their King, and the whole city was stirred.

## Coloring Page Prompt

Joyful black-and-white line art for ages 3–8. Jesus riding calmly on a small donkey. Happy children and people waving palm branches and laying coats on the road. A big crowd cheering with “Hosanna!” in the air. Gentle hills and the city walls of Jerusalem in the background. Celebratory, peaceful, and reverent feeling. Clean bold lines with large open spaces.

---

## Read-Along Flow

Color the joyful crowd and Jesus on the donkey → listen to the story of the King who comes in peace and receives our praise → remember that Jesus is our King, and we can welcome Him with our whole hearts every day.

## Quiz — 4 Questions (with helpful scaffolding)

**1. What did Jesus ride into Jerusalem on?**
- A) A big white horse
- B) A humble donkey
- C) A camel
- D) He walked

*Correct: B*  
Wrong answers scaffolding:
- If A: “Kings who wanted to show power rode war horses. Jesus chose a gentle donkey to show He came in peace.”
- If C: “It was a donkey, just as the prophets had said long ago.”
- If D: “He rode so everyone could see Him clearly as He entered the city.”

**2. What did the people shout as Jesus rode by?**
- A) “Go away!”
- B) “Hosanna to the Son of David! Blessed is He who comes in the name of the Lord!”
- C) “We don’t want a king!”
- D) “This is boring”

*Correct: B*  
Wrong answers scaffolding:
- If A: “The crowd was full of joy and praise. They were welcoming Jesus as their King.”
- If C: “They were celebrating! They believed He was the promised King from God.”
- If D: “It was the most exciting day many of them had ever seen.”

**3. Why were some of the religious leaders unhappy about the cheering?**
- A) Because they couldn’t hear each other talk
- B) Because they didn’t believe Jesus was the King sent from God
- C) Because it was too quiet
- D) Because they wanted more palm branches for themselves

*Correct: B*  
Wrong answers scaffolding:
- If A: “They weren’t bothered by the noise. They were upset that the people were praising Jesus as the Messiah.”
- If C: “The city was very loud with praise. That was exactly what they didn’t like.”
- If D: “It wasn’t about the branches. It was about who the people said Jesus was.”

**4. What does this story help us remember about Jesus?**
- A) That He only likes quiet people
- B) That He is our humble King who is worthy of our joyful praise
- C) That donkeys are the best animals
- D) That we should never cheer in public

*Correct: B*  
Wrong answers scaffolding:
- If A: “Jesus welcomed the children’s cheers and the people’s praise. He loves our joyful worship.”
- If C: “The donkey shows us Jesus came gently and in peace, not to force His way.”
- If D: “This story invites us to praise Jesus with our whole hearts, just like the crowd did.”
